Amazon Mechanical Turk (MTurk)

Amazon Mechanical Turk is a crowdsourcing platform that connects businesses with people who perform human intelligence tasks (HITs) that are difficult for computers to do reliably.

Simple definition

Amazon Mechanical Turk = Humans perform small tasks that help organizations collect, label, or validate data.

Think of it as:

Company
   ↓
Posts task
   ↓
Mechanical Turk
   ↓
Human workers
   ↓
Complete task
   ↓
Results
Why is Mechanical Turk useful for AI/ML? ⭐

ML models need high-quality training data.

But raw data often needs humans to label or validate it.

For example, suppose you have 100,000 images:

Image
 ↓
Human worker
 ↓
"Cat"
"Dog"
"Car"
"Person"

These labels can then be used as training data for a supervised ML model.

Human Intelligence Tasks (HITs)

A task posted on Mechanical Turk is called a Human Intelligence Task (HIT).

Examples:

Label images
Categorize text
Transcribe short audio
Validate information
Answer surveys
Identify objects
Compare search results
Check whether an ML-generated result is correct

Example:

HIT:
"Is there a person in this image?"

        ↓

Human worker

        ↓

YES

cats vs dogs.

Labeled images

Mechanical Turk vs Amazon SageMaker Ground Truth

This is an important distinction for the exam.

Mechanical Turk

Crowdsourcing platform providing access to human workers.

SageMaker Ground Truth

AWS service for data labeling workflows, which can use human labelers, including Mechanical Turk in appropriate workflows.

Conceptually:

Data
 ↓
SageMaker Ground Truth
 ↓
Labeling workflow
 ↓
Human labelers
 ↓
Labeled dataset

So if the question specifically asks for:

A crowdsourcing platform where humans perform small tasks

→ Amazon Mechanical Turk

If it asks for:

An AWS service to build and manage data-labeling workflows

→ Amazon SageMaker Ground Truth

Mechanical Turk vs Amazon Comprehend

Don't confuse human data labeling with automatic NLP.

Mechanical Turk
Task → HUMAN → Result
Comprehend
Text → ML/NLP SERVICE → Analysis

For example:

"Have humans classify 10,000 customer comments into categories."

→ Mechanical Turk

"Automatically determine sentiment in customer comments."

→ Amazon Comprehend

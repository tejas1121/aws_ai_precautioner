1. What is AI Governance?

AI governance is the set of policies, processes, controls, and responsibilities used to ensure that AI systems are developed and used safely, responsibly, ethically, and in compliance with requirements.

Think of it as:

Governance = rules + controls + responsibility for managing AI

For example, an organization may establish rules such as:

Who can create AI applications?
Which data can be used?
Who approves a model before production?
How should models be monitored?
What happens when a model becomes inaccurate?
How are AI risks documented?
Who is accountable for an AI system?
2. Importance of AI Governance

Why do we need governance?

① Risk Management

AI can create risks such as:

Bias
Hallucination
Privacy violations
Security vulnerabilities
Incorrect decisions

Governance establishes processes to identify, assess, and manage these risks.

② Compliance

Governance helps organizations ensure that AI usage follows:

Laws
Regulations
Industry standards
Internal policies
③ Accountability

There should be clear ownership.

For example:

AI Model
   ↓
Model Owner
   ↓
Responsible for:
- Performance
- Risk
- Monitoring
- Updates

Without governance, it can be unclear who is responsible when something goes wrong.

④ Consistency

Imagine 20 teams building AI applications independently.

Without governance:

Team A → one security policy
Team B → another policy
Team C → no documentation
Team D → different data rules

Governance establishes common standards.

⑤ Trust

Good governance improves confidence that AI systems are:

Reliable
Fair
Secure
Transparent
Properly managed

4. Governance Strategies

A governance strategy defines how an organization will manage AI systematically.

A typical strategy includes:

Identify AI system
       ↓
Assess risk
       ↓
Define policies
       ↓
Control data/access
       ↓
Evaluate model
       ↓
Approve model
       ↓
Deploy
       ↓
Monitor
       ↓
Audit
       ↓
Improve / Retire
Important elements

1. Policies

Define acceptable AI usage.

2. Roles and responsibilities

Determine who owns and approves systems.

3. Risk assessment

Identify potential risks before deployment.

4. Documentation

Document models, datasets, intended use, limitations, evaluations, etc.

5. Monitoring

Continuously monitor deployed systems.

6. Human oversight

Keep humans involved when appropriate, especially for high-impact decisions.

7. Access control

Use least privilege and appropriate IAM controls.

5. Data Governance

Now we move from AI governance to data governance.

Definition

Data governance is the framework for managing data so that it is:

Available
Accurate
Consistent
Secure
Properly used
Properly managed throughout its lifecycle

Think:

Data Governance = rules and responsibilities for managing data

6. Why Data Governance Matters for AI

AI models are heavily dependent on their data.

Bad data
   ↓
Bad training
   ↓
Bad model
   ↓
Bad decisions

This is essentially:

Garbage in → garbage out

Poor data governance can cause:

Biased models
Incorrect predictions
Privacy violations
Poor data quality
Compliance problems
Data leakage

8. Data Management

Data management is the broader practice of collecting, storing, organizing, processing, protecting, and maintaining data.

For example:

Data Sources
    ↓
Collection
    ↓
Storage
    ↓
Processing
    ↓
Analysis / ML
    ↓
Archiving / Deletion
Governance vs Management

This distinction is important.

Data Governance

What rules should we follow? Who is responsible?

Data Management

How do we actually collect, store, process, protect, and maintain the data?

Example:

Governance:

Customer medical data must only be accessible to authorized employees.

Management:

Configure the storage system and IAM permissions so only those employees can access it.

9. Data Lineage ⭐

This is a very important concept.

Data lineage means tracking the origin, movement, transformations, and destination of data throughout its lifecycle.

In simple words:

Where did this data come from, what happened to it, and where did it go?

Example

Suppose an ML model predicts customer churn.

The data may travel like this:

CRM Database
     ↓
Customer Data
     ↓
ETL / Transformation
     ↓
S3 Dataset
     ↓
Feature Engineering
     ↓
Training Dataset
     ↓
ML Model
     ↓
Prediction

Data lineage allows you to trace this journey.

10. Why Data Lineage Matters
① Troubleshooting

Suppose the model suddenly performs poorly.

You can trace:

Model
 ↓
Training data
 ↓
Transformation
 ↓
Original source

and identify where the problem originated.

② Compliance

If an organization needs to know:

"Where did this customer's information go?"

Data lineage can help trace its movement and transformations.

③ Data Quality

If a dataset contains incorrect values, lineage helps identify:

Which source or transformation introduced the problem?

④ Impact Analysis

Suppose a database column changes.

Lineage helps determine:

Which datasets, models, reports, or applications depend on this column?

11. Data Lineage vs Data Lifecycle

Don't confuse them.

Data Lifecycle

Describes stages of data:

Create → Store → Use → Archive → Delete
Data Lineage

Describes data's journey and transformations:

Source
 ↓
Transformation
 ↓
Dataset
 ↓
Model
 ↓
Output

Memory:

Lifecycle = what stages data goes through

Lineage = where data came from and where it went
